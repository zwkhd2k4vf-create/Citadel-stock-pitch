## Weak spots in the model to fix or prepare to defend

1. **Costs and fuel account for almost a third of the gap to consensus.** In the bridge, $107M of the $342M comes from costs and fuel rather than either thesis.
   - Your case has unit costs ex fuel +1.0% in 2027; the consensus case has −0.5%.
   - Your case has fuel at $800/t; the consensus case has $760/t.
   - Your +1% is above the 1H26 run-rate (about flat y/y) and the 3Q26 guide (−0.9%), and it ignores the $225M savings program listed under Risks.
   - With consensus costs, the theses alone give about **$12.16 (−19%)**. With flat 2027 unit costs, value is **$11.01 (−27%)**.
   - v12 now says this openly in Valuation, which works better than having a judge find it. Either justify the +1% (2025 was +1.1%) or move to flat. The short still works either way.
2. **The reverse-DCF rate doesn't match.** The `Model>>>` tab quotes 8.5% ($2.93B), the memo and chart 1 use 9% ($3.0B), and the model's WACC is 9.05% ($3.05B). Pick one; v12 uses 9%. Chart 1 shows "$3.00B" where the model gives $3.04–3.05B.
3. **Net debt is hard-coded in one place.** The EV/EBITDA value uses a typed-in $15.8B, while the DCF uses the model's $15.88B. That's about $0.18/share. Link both to `Operating Model!H88`, or note why they differ.
4. **The multiple–net yield link only holds since 2024.** r = 0.87 over 10 quarters since 2024, but r = 0.07 in 2016–19. v12 says "since 2024". Be ready for this question.
5. **The deposit back-test is weaker than the headline.** Correlation is 0.52 with next-quarter net yield and 0.65 with the next two quarters over the full sample; it is 0.86 only since 2024. The 80% hit rate is real, but the lead time is long and varies.
6. **The bull case is above Street EBITDA.** The consensus case needs $2.78B of EBITDA, above the Street-implied $2.69B, because it is calibrated to consensus EPS after the higher interest guide. Worth one sentence of prep in case a judge asks why the bull case is above Street EBITDA.
